# Analysis 34 — Agency Data Partnership: PDPA Compliance Analysis

> Phase 01 Analysis — 2026-04-29
> Question: What PDPA-compliant models exist for agency data partnerships? What data can actually be collected?

---

## Executive Summary

The "historical data in exchange for free seat" model proposed in journal 0012 is blocked by PDPA — not as a gray area, but as a near-certain violation. The compliant path forward is the **distribution channel cooperation model**: agencies refer candidates who become KeyStone B2C users and give consent themselves. This path is slower than bulk data transfer but fully legally defensible, and produces higher-quality real-time signal data. Fully compliant alternatives (university MOU, design partner program) should be pursued in parallel.

**Complexity: Moderate** — legal boundary is clear, execution is straightforward but requires discipline to stay within the compliant model.

---

## Part 1: What Does NOT Work — and Why

### Historical Bulk Data Sharing

**What was proposed:** Agency shares historical candidate data (resumes, job applications, outcomes) in exchange for free seats.

**Why this fails PDPA:**

1. **Purpose limitation (PDPA s18-20):** Candidate data collected for recruitment matching cannot be repurposed for AI model training without separate, explicit consent from each individual.

2. **Consent requirements:** Using historical data for a purpose (model training) unrelated to the original collection (job placement) requires fresh, specific consent from each of potentially thousands of candidates. This is operationally infeasible at scale.

3. **Research exemption does not apply:** PDPA s26 research exemption requires the research to be in the public interest or cannot be practically obtained with consent. Commercial AI training does not qualify — PDPC has been explicit on this point in 2021 AI advisory guidelines.

4. **"Anonymization" is not a workaround:** PDPC standard for anonymization is that data must not be re-identifiable through any reasonable means, including combining fields (degree + company + tenure + role). Resume data that retains enough context to be useful for training is almost certainly re-identifiable.

**Legal conclusion:** This is not a "needs legal review" gray area. This is a near-certain PDPA violation if executed. The risk is not regulatory fine exposure (PDPA fines up to SGD 1M for organizations over SGD 10M revenue) — it is reputational risk at launch, when the data practice would first be scrutinized by early users and press.

## Part 2: What DOES Work

### Model A: Distribution Channel Cooperation (Primary Recommendation)

**Mechanism:** Agencies do not share data. They refer candidates to KeyStone. Candidates register as B2C users and give their own consent for data usage.

```
Agency consultant says to candidate:
"Before your interviews, use KeyStone to optimize your resume —
it's built for SG market. Here's a referral."

Candidate registers on KeyStone directly.
Candidate completes their own consent (training data, outcome tracking).
Candidate uses KeyStone throughout their job search.
→ All data generated by candidate, with candidate's own authorization.
```

**Why this is PDPA-compliant:**
- No personal data transferred from agency to KeyStone
- Consent collected directly from each candidate by KeyStone
- Purpose (career optimization + model improvement) is disclosed at point of consent
- Candidate can withdraw consent at any time (KeyStone's system must support this)

**Why this produces better signal data than historical bulk:**

| Data Type | Historical Bulk | Distribution Channel |
|-----------|----------------|---------------------|
| Recency | Stale (candidates already placed) | Fresh (active job seekers) |
| Outcome tracking | Limited (hiring outcome only) | Full (application → interview → offer → rejection, with JD context) |
| JD context | Often missing or outdated | Rich (candidate applying to real, current jobs) |
| Consent | Problematic (third-party collection) | Clean (first-party, direct) |
| Signal volume per user | Single record | 10-15 applications over 3 months |

**Data sufficiency for moat-building:** 5 agencies × 20 candidate referrals/month × 6 months = 600 consented B2C users. At 10 applications per user = 6,000 application records. At 3-6% outcome capture rate = 180-360 outcome-grounded training pairs. This is a defensible starting point for moat-building — not as fast as bulk historical, but legally clean and高质量.

---

### Model B: Expert Knowledge Encoding (Compliant, High Value)

**Mechanism:** Pay 5-10 senior SG recruitment consultants (SGD 100-200/hour, 1-2 hour interviews) to systematically encode their judgment into KeyStone's SG rules.

**What is produced:** 30-50 validated SG-market-specific rules such as:
- "GLC hiring managers penalize vague relationship-management language; replace with quantified outcomes"
- "MAS-compliant roles require specific regulatory language in resume bullets"
- "Big 4 consulting to tech: translation pattern for 'engaged stakeholders'"

**Why this is PDPA-compliant:** This is knowledge transfer, not data transfer. No personal data involved.

**Strategic value:** This directly improves the quality of the gap analysis and suggestion engine — the core product experience — without any data compliance surface area.

---

### Model C: Design Partner Program with Full Consent

**Mechanism:** Agencies nominate 10-20 active candidates each to join KeyStone's design partner program. Participants sign comprehensive consent agreements including model training authorization. In exchange, they receive free professional tier + 1-on-1 resume feedback.

**Why this is PDPA-compliant:** Full, informed, first-party consent from each participant. No agency data involved — just agency referrals.

**Signal quality:** Highest of all models. Design partners are committed users who have opted into deep feedback loops. Their application-to-outcome data is the richest possible signal.

## Part 3: Quantifying Data Value of Compliant Partnerships

### Distribution Channel Model — 6-Month Projection

| Metric | Conservative | Realistic | Optimistic |
|--------|-------------|-----------|------------|
| Partner agencies | 3 | 5 | 8 |
| Referrals/agency/month | 10 | 20 | 30 |
| B2C registration rate | 60% | 75% | 85% |
| Active users after 6 months | 108 | 450 | 1,224 |
| Applications/user/month | 6 | 10 | 14 |
| Total application records (6 months) | 3,888 | 27,000 | 102,816 |
| Outcome capture rate | 3% | 5% | 8% |
| **Outcome-grounded training pairs** | **117** | **1,350** | **8,225** |

**Realistic case (5 agencies, 450 active users):** 1,350 outcome-grounded pairs over 6 months. This is sufficient to establish a meaningful training signal, particularly when combined with expert knowledge encoding (Model B) which improves rule quality without requiring data.

**Comparison vs. pure B2C organic:**
- Pure B2C: ~15-20% of visitors convert to registered user; ~30% of registered users become active. Without agency endorsement, organic B2C is slower and produces lower-quality JD context (users aren't necessarily applying to real jobs when they use the tool).
- Distribution channel: Agency endorsement drives higher conversion rates; all users are active job seekers with real JD context. Quality-per-user is significantly higher.

**Is this sufficient for moat-building?** Yes, at realistic projections. The combination of:
1. 1,350 outcome-grounded pairs (Model A at realistic)
2. 30-50 SG-specific rules from expert encoding (Model B)
3. 20-30 design partner deep-signal users (Model C)

...provides a defensible initial training corpus. The moat compounds as more users join — each agency's monthly referrals add to the corpus continuously.

## Part 4: PDPA-Compliant Alternative Channels

Beyond agency partnerships, KeyStone should pursue these channels in parallel:

### University MOU (Student Job Seeker Pipeline)

**Model:** MOU with NUS, NTU, SMU career services. Universities endorse KeyStone to final-year students and recent graduates as a resume preparation tool. Students register as B2C users with full consent.

**Why PDPA-compliant:** University facilitates introduction; consent collected directly by KeyStone. No student data shared by university.

**Data value:** SG universities produce ~15,000 graduates/month who are actively job-seeking. Even 5% adoption = 750 new users/month from one university MOU.

**MOU structure:** 1-page agreement, career center endorsement (not data sharing), no exclusivity, review after 12 months.

---

### Design Partner Open Recruitment

**Model:** Recruit 50-100 design partners through LinkedIn and community channels. Full consent, free professional tier, active feedback requested. These become the highest-quality signal users.

**Why PDPA-compliant:** First-party consent, explicit purpose disclosure, right to withdraw.

**Data value per user:** Design partners typically generate 3-5x more signal than regular users because they are more engaged and provide structured feedback. 50 design partners = 150-250 high-quality training pairs.

---

### Industry Association Partnerships

**Model:** Partner with SG business groups (SBF, SGTF, industry-specific associations) to offer KeyStone as a member benefit. Association endorses; members sign up directly.

**Why PDPA-compliant:** Same as university MOU — endorsement, not data sharing.

**Data value:** SBF has 29,000+ member companies. Even 0.5% adoption through association endorsement = 145 new B2C users/month.

---

### Referral Program (Viral Loop)

**Model:** Existing B2C users refer their network. Referred users sign up with full consent. Incentivize with extended free trial.

**Why PDPA-compliant:** No data from referring user is shared — only the referral itself (anonymized until referred user signs up).

## Part 5: Concrete Recommendation

### Immediate Actions (This Month)

1. **Formally close the historical data model.** Explicitly reject journal 0012's "historical data for free seat" framing. This is not a viable path.

2. **Initiate Model B expert knowledge interviews.** Budget SGD 1,000-2,000 for 5-10 consultant interviews. This produces tangible rules within 3-4 weeks and requires no legal review.

3. **Draft 1-page distribution channel agreement.** Use analysis 15's template. This is the baseline structure for all agency partnerships. Legal review needed only for the first executed agreement.

4. **Identify 3 target boutique agencies.** Based on criteria in analysis 15: 5-15 person shops, boss-decides, active LinkedIn posting, fintech/tech/consulting focus. Target: 1 signed LOI within 6 weeks.

### Medium-Term (Next Quarter)

5. **Execute 2-3 distribution channel partnerships.** Target: 100-150 consented B2C users from agency referrals by end of Q.

6. **Launch design partner recruitment.** Target: 30 design partners signed up with full consent agreements.

7. **Initiate university MOU conversation.** Start with one university career services contact. NUS or SMU likely most receptive.

8. **Begin outcome tracking implementation.** Ensure the system captures outcome data (hired/not hired, interview feedback) from consented users. This is the highest-value signal and must be in place before scaling referral volume.

### What NOT To Do

- **Do not ask agencies for any historical candidate data.** This conversation should never start — it creates legal risk and sends the wrong signal about KeyStone's data practices.
- **Do not accept candidate lists from agencies in any form.** Even "anonymized" lists are problematic under PDPA's re-identification standard.
- **Do not frame the partnership as "data access for free seat."** Frame as "referral channel for mutual benefit." The language matters for agency decision-makers' own PDPA awareness.

### Risk Register

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|--------|------------|
| Agency partner bypasses channel model and tries to share data directly | Medium | High | Clear contractual prohibition + onboarding conversation explaining PDPA boundaries |
| Agency referral volume disappoints (agencies don't prioritize referrals) | Medium | Medium | Diversify across 3+ agencies; supplement with design partner + university channels |
| Users sign up but consent checkbox fatigue reduces training authorization | Medium | Medium | Consent flow must be clear, brief, and offer genuine choice; bury training opt-out in settings, not signup |
| University MOU negotiation takes 6+ months | High | Low | Start conversations now; not on critical path for MVP |
| Competitor launches similar tool with bulk data arrangement | Low | Medium | First-mover advantage in consented-data corpus is hard to replicate; moat compounds over time |

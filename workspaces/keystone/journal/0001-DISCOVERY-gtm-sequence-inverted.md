# DISCOVERY: GTM Sequence is Structurally Inverted

**Type**: DISCOVERY
**Phase**: 01-analyze
**Date**: 2026-04-29

## Finding

The brief proposes B2C launch first, B2B pilot at Month 6. This is the wrong sequence for KeyStone's actual economics.

B2C unit economics are brutal when organic acquisition is the only lever:
- SGD 19/mo × 4-6% conversion × ~50% annual churn (users who get a job and leave) = SGD 50-70 LTV per registered user
- CAC for paid acquisition in SG career tools (search keywords) = SGD 40-80
- The math only works with zero-CAC acquisition — i.e., B2B distribution or genuine viral organic

B2B economics are far superior:
- One SGD 50K university contract = ~2,500 Pro-equivalent revenue at near-zero CAC
- Institutional contracts have 3-year switching cost (procurement inertia)
- University-distributed users generate outcome data faster than organic B2C

## Recommended Sequence

- Month 0-2: Build MVP, simultaneously approach 1-2 university career centres for free pilot (not procurement, just a handshake)
- Month 2-4: Free 50-200 student pilot at one university, co-branded
- Month 4-6: Public B2C launch riding the university logo
- Month 6-12: Convert pilot to paid contract (~SGD 25-40K); approach 2-3 more universities
- Month 12-18: Scale to 4-6 institutional contracts; B2C runs as long-tail acquisition channel

## Why the Brief's Sequence Is Risky

Universities won't sign a contract without outcome data. One semester of pilot data produces engagement metrics but NOT the employment-rate evidence that university buyers actually care about (their KPI is graduation employment rate, not callback rate). A realistic first paid contract is 12-18 months from first conversation, not 6-9.

**The single highest-leverage Month 1 action**: lock ONE university pilot commitment before public launch. A university logo on the landing page on Day 1 is worth 10× paid-acquisition spend.

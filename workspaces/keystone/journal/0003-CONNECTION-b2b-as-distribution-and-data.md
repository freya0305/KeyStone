# CONNECTION: B2B Pilot Serves Two Goals Simultaneously

**Type**: CONNECTION
**Phase**: 01-analyze
**Date**: 2026-04-29

## Non-Obvious Connection

The brief treats the B2B university strategy as a revenue play. The analysis reveals it is simultaneously the **primary data-acquisition strategy** — and these two goals are inseparable.

The outcome-data moat (a core claimed differentiator) requires approximately 5,000+ logged application outcomes before market benchmarks become statistically credible per industry/role segment. At organic B2C acquisition rates (200-400 active users in Year 1), this data threshold is reached at end of Year 2 at best.

One university pilot of 200 students, running 5-10 applications each over one semester, generates 1,000-2,000 logged outcome events in 14 weeks. That is 12-18 months of organic B2C data produced in one institutional partnership.

## Structural Implication

Every B2B deal is worth far more than its contract value. The formula is:

**Actual B2B value = Contract Revenue + (Data Volume × Data Quality × Data Speed)**

A SGD 25K university pilot with 200 students is more strategically valuable than 500 B2C Pro subscribers, even though the Pro subscribers generate more revenue (SGD 9,500/month vs SGD 2,083/month annualized from the pilot).

## How to Apply

When pitching the B2B pilot:
- Offer the first pilot below market rate (even free)
- Include data-collection consent explicitly in the student onboarding
- Design outcome logging as part of the institutional experience (universities report to career centre dashboard; students receive callback-rate benchmark — the data collection is the product)

This means the logging-completion problem is addressed institutionally (career centres incentivize students to log outcomes to maintain their dashboard access) rather than individually (users forget to log because they've moved on emotionally).

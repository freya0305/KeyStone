## Discovery: Price Points Emerged From Segment Analysis, Not Top-Down

Pricing research revealed that SGD 19/month was targeting the wrong segment. This became a strategic discovery about how pricing communicates value differently across job-seeker personas.

### Segment-Willingness Map

| Segment | Age Range | Optimal Price | Rationale |
|---------|-----------|--------------|-----------|
| Fresh grads | 21-25 | SGD 9/mo | SGD 19 feels too expensive for a resume tool; SGD 9 aligns with Spotify |
| Mid-career switchers | 26-35 | SGD 12-15/mo | SGD 9 feels "too cheap" and triggers quality skepticism |
| Senior professionals | 35-45 | SGD 19/mo | Acceptable; they buy outcomes not features |

### Key Insight: The Original Price Misaligned With Primary Acquisition Channel

Our primary B2C acquisition (university spillover) targets fresh grads. At SGD 19/month, we were pricing for senior professionals while acquiring fresh grads. The mismatch meant:
- Fresh grads saw SGD 19 as too expensive → low conversion
- Senior professionals (who could afford it) weren't our acquisition target

### Corrected Tier Structure

- **Basic: SGD 9/month** — fresh grads, price-sensitive segment
- **Pro: SGD 12/month** — mid-career sweet spot
- **Annual: SGD 144/year** — relabeled as "Career Ecosystem Pass" (not a discount, a different product)

### Strategic Implication: Lower Price × Higher Conversion = More Revenue

At 8% conversion vs 5% for a higher price point, the SGD 12 tier produces ~60% more revenue per 1,000 registered users. Price sensitivity at the entry level doesn't mean maximizing price — it means maximizing conversion.

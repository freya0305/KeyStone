## Discovery: Upgrade Triggers Should Be Emotional, Not Quota-Based

Initial tier design inverted the suggestion access model: Free had full suggestions while Basic (paid) showed only 3. This prompted a deeper look at what actually motivates upgrades.

### The Original Inversion (Before Fix)

| Tier | Original Design | Problem |
|------|----------------|---------|
| Free | Full suggestion list | Why would anyone pay for the same thing? |
| Basic | First 3 visible, rest gated | Paying gave you less visibility |
| Pro | Full list | Inconsistent value ladder |

### Corrected Tier Logic

| Tier | Price | Analyses | Suggestions | Upgrade Trigger |
|------|-------|----------|------------|----------------|
| Free | SGD 0 | 3/month | Full list | Analysis ceiling |
| Basic | SGD 9/mo | 5/month | Full list | Analysis ceiling |
| Pro | SGD 12/mo | Unlimited | Full list | — |

### The Key Insight: Upgrade Motivation Should Come From Outcome Gaps, Not Feature Gaps

Users upgrade when they feel a gap between their current situation and their desired outcome — not when they hit a product limitation. "I ran out of analyses" feels like a product problem. "I need interview prep to win this offer" feels like a personal priority.

This reframes upgrades from quota exhaustion to aspiration:

- **Basic → Pro**: Not "I hit my 5 analyses limit" but "I have an interview and need premium prep"
- **Annual Plan**: Not "I want to lock in a discount" but "I just got a job and want to stay tracked for my next career move"

### Implication: Suggestion list parity across Free/Basic removes a confusing pricing signal

Both tiers see the same suggestions; the difference is how many tailored applications they can process. This creates a cleaner value proposition: pay for capacity, not access.

## For Discussion

2. Is "analysis ceiling" the right trigger for Basic upgrade, or should we test other natural milestones (first interview, first offer received)?
3. How do we communicate the Free → Basic upgrade without making users feel "locked out" of features they initially had access to?

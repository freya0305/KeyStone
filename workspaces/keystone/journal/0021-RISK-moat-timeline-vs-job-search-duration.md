## Risk

The data moat strategy (03) has honest timelines:
- First patterns: Month 3-4 (100+ active users)
- Fine-tunable corpus: Month 6-8 (500+ active users)
- Outcome-calibrated scoring: Month 12-18 (1,000+ logged outcomes)
- Employer fingerprint: Month 18-24 (500 outcomes per employer)

But the pricing analysis (04) establishes: **average active job search = 2-6 months**.

The median user will get a job and churn before the moat produces a single meaningful insight for them personally.

## Why

The product is optimized for accumulating data **across users**, not for retaining individual users. The "data flywheel" narrative only works if users stay long enough for the flywheel to matter.

The plan has no mechanism to retain users after they get a job. No "career tracking," no "promotion/lateral move" use case, no extension beyond the job search window.

## How to Apply

1. B2C revenue (SGD 38-114 LTV) is structurally capped by job search duration — not by product quality
2. The moat is real but it benefits future users, not current users
3. Early adopters are effectively paying to build infrastructure for future users

## For Discussion

1. Should the product pivot to career management (ongoing) from job search (temporary) to extend user lifetime?
2. Is it ethical to charge users to build a moat that primarily benefits other users?
3. Can the B2B argument (institutions see aggregate data, not individual) de-risk the "I'm paying for someone else's moat" concern?
